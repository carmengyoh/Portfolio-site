TL;DR

The 30-second version

PROBLEM

Merchants couldn't easily compare multiple funding products, or trust they weren't being steered towards the biggest amount.

MY ROLE

Led the design end to end: research synthesis, personas, journey design, prototyping and validation.

KEY MOVE

One flexible journey that adapts between independent comparison and guided recommendation, instead of three separate flows.

WHAT I LEARNED

Multi-product design is a decision-design problem, not a navigation one. Next time I'd build in earlier, lighter research checkpoints to catch assumptions before committing to a direction.

01

Overview

As Liberis moved towards offering merchants more than one funding product, the job was no longer just helping someone complete an application. It was helping them choose, without the experience quietly steering them towards the largest amount available.

How might we help merchants understand, compare and confidently choose the right funding product without overwhelming them or steering the decision?

What I owned

  • Framed the customer decision, not just the UI
  • Synthesised research into behavioural personas
  • Designed and prototyped the journey concepts
  • Ran validation and refined before handoff

Who I worked with

I worked closely with [PM / sales / research / compliance / commercial] to balance merchant understanding, product eligibility and commercial priorities against the practical constraints of the future experience.

Research: how merchants decide

Behavioural persona synthesis

I grouped findings around behaviours and decision criteria rather than demographics. Some merchants wanted to decide independently; others wanted the experience to interpret the options for them.

Behaviour What they need Design implication
Self-directed Clear facts, freedom to decide without intervention. Accessible comparison, no forced recommendation.
Guidance-seeking Help interpreting which option fits their goals. Progressive questions, plain-language guidance.
Cost-led optimiser Enough detail to compare cost & flexibility. Key terms visible, deeper detail on demand.
KEY INSIGHT

The same information didn't serve every merchant equally. The goal wasn't three separate journeys, but one experience that could flex between independent comparison and meaningful guidance.

03

Design & key decisions

I moved from lo-fi journey sketches to a high-fidelity prototype, testing each stage against the design principle: give merchants enough structure to decide confidently, while preserving their sense of control.

Decision 1: [name it]

Comparison without overload

Structured the comparison so the immediate decision used a small set of meaningful differences, with deeper cost and repayment detail available on demand. Useful to both first-time and experienced borrowers.

Decision 2

Explaining recommendations

Where the experience guided a merchant towards an option, I made the reasoning visible, so it read as understandable rather than opaque, with an easy route to review alternatives.

Reflection
