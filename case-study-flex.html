Overview

Flex Advance introduced a new way for merchants to access funding through a reusable limit rather than one fixed advance. In Partner Hub, agents needed to create the merchant's account, complete the funding assessment, establish the reusable Flex Advance limit and take the first advance on the merchant's behalf. These stages did not always happen in one sitting. An agent could pause the journey while the merchant completed an external step, then return later and continue.

How might we help partner agents set up, assess and activate a new Flex Advance account on behalf of a merchant while keeping progress and next steps clear when the journey pauses?

I owned the journey end to end: mapping the agent flow, defining the information hierarchy and status model, designing the detailed screens and edge cases, then refining the funding assessment screen using findings from pre-launch prototype testing.

WHY IT WAS HARD

Agents could complete some steps themselves, but other steps depended on the merchant providing information or connecting Open Banking. Partner Hub therefore needed to show what had already been completed, who needed to act next and whether the agent could continue immediately or needed to return later.

The process

Step #1: Mapping the Flex Advance funding journey

Because Flex Advance was a new product, there was no established Partner Hub journey to reuse. I mapped merchant, agent and system actions before moving into detailed screen design. This showed where agents could progress independently, where merchant participation was required and what status Partner Hub needed to communicate when the journey paused.

Step #2: Designing for progress, pauses and the first advance

The Flex Advance journey could pause while a merchant completed Open Banking or while an agent gathered missing information. I designed the status and progress model so an agent returning to the account could understand what had already happened, why the journey had stopped and what needed to happen next.

Explaining the first advance

The first advance wasn't simply an amount-selection step. Agents needed a clear way to explain what taking funding would mean for the merchant, whether they were together or discussing it remotely. I designed an interactive calculator that let agents explore an advance amount and immediately see the corresponding fixed fee, percentage of daily sales and total repayment.

Establishing the reusable limit and taking the first advance were treated as separate decisions. Agents could first review the available limit, then choose an amount and explain its cost before confirming the first advance.

Step #3: Testing the prototype with partner agents

Before launch, I ran moderated sessions with partner sales agents using a clickable prototype. I created the discussion guide and scenarios, facilitated each session and synthesised the findings. The sessions combined scenario-based walkthroughs, think-aloud observation and questions about how agents prepared for and supported merchant conversations.

Agents needed to pause and resume without losing work. Some journeys could not be completed in one sitting, particularly when a merchant needed to complete Open Banking. Agents needed confidence that they could leave the application, return later and understand exactly where to continue.

Revenue needed to match how agents spoke with merchants. The original journey asked for annual revenue only, but agents often discussed revenue in monthly terms. The revised input allowed them to switch between monthly and annual figures while keeping the assessment consistent.

Indicative conversations and application data served different purposes. Agents wanted to discuss a possible funding amount without committing the merchant to an application. They also needed a route for merchants who could not complete Open Banking but still wanted to proceed using self-declared figures.

Step #4: Refining the funding assessment screen

The original funding assessment screen gave Open Banking and estimated figures similar visual weight, even though they served different purposes. The revised screen separated a non-committal funding preview from the routes that created or updated an application.

Three routes became clearer. Open Banking became the primary application route because it used verified revenue data to establish an accurate Flex Advance limit. Funding preview became a separate, non-committal tool so agents could enter estimated figures and discuss a possible amount without saving it to the merchant's account or starting an application. Can't do Open Banking remained an application route, taking the agent to a dedicated estimated-figures screen where self-declared monthly or annual revenue could be used to create a quote linked to the application.

Processor selection also moved into its own step, showing only processors associated with the merchant rather than asking agents to search through irrelevant options.

Outcome

The MVP established an end-to-end Flex Advance funding journey in Partner Hub, allowing partner agents to move merchants from account creation through to establishing a reusable limit and taking the first advance. Pre-launch testing helped refine a critical part of that journey by making Open Banking clearer, reducing processor-selection errors and giving agents a more appropriate way to explore indicative funding with merchants.

RESULT

A shipped 0-to-1 Flex Advance journey used by partner agents in production, with the funding assessment screen refined through direct observation and feedback before launch.

Reflection

An indicative conversation, an evidence-based assessment and a confirmed first advance serve different needs and shouldn't be presented as interchangeable routes. Separating levels of certainty is a design decision, not a detail.

The redesign was stronger because testing showed that agents were trying to do two different jobs: discuss a possible funding amount without starting an application, and progress a merchant through an assessment when they were ready. Separating Funding Preview from the Open Banking and self-declared application routes made those intentions clearer. It also reinforced that Partner Hub needed to support journeys that pause and resume, not assume every application would be completed in one sitting. With more time, I would continue testing these return states to make sure agents can always understand what has happened and what needs to happen next.
