TL;DR

The 30-second version

PROBLEM

Partner agents needed to set up a brand-new funding product, live in front of a merchant, across a long journey with no existing pattern to lean on.

MY ROLE

Designed the journey 0 to 1, then returned after launch to redesign the assessment experience using partner feedback.

KEY MOVE

Made progress and status explicit across a long dependent journey, and separated the Flex limit from the first drawdown.

WHAT I LEARNED

Shipping isn't the finish line. The strongest decisions came from watching agents struggle with the live product, not from getting it right the first time.

01

Overview

Flex introduced a new way for merchants to access funding through a reusable limit. Partner agents needed to create the merchant's account, complete the funding assessment, generate the Flex limit and take the first drawdown on the merchant's behalf, all inside Partner Hub, all in one live conversation.

How might we help partner agents set up, assess and activate a new Flex account on behalf of a merchant without losing clarity across a long, dependent journey?

What I owned

  • Mapped the complete agent journey, account creation to first drawdown
  • Defined the information hierarchy and status model across the flow
  • Designed detailed screens and edge cases for engineering handoff
  • Returned after launch to redesign based on live feedback

Why it was difficult

The journey crossed account creation, business information, funding assessment, limit generation and drawdown, with dependencies on merchant data, payment processors and eligibility rules. For agents, it had to feel efficient and explainable mid-conversation. For Liberis, it had to collect the right information and avoid accidental progression when evidence was missing.

02

Phase 1: building the core journey

Because Flex was a new product, there was no established Partner Hub pattern to reuse. I began by mapping merchant, agent and system actions across the whole journey, before touching a single screen. That made the dependencies visible: where an agent could progress independently, where merchant participation was required, and where a system status needed explaining.

Decision 1

Make progress and status explicit

The journey contained steps that couldn't always be completed in one sitting. I designed the experience so agents could see what was done, what was blocking progress, and what to do next, so an application never felt like a pile of disconnected forms.

Decision 2

Separate limit setup from first drawdown

A Flex limit and an advance taken from it are related, but they aren't the same commitment. I structured the journey so the agent understood the available limit, reviewed the chosen advance, and completed the first drawdown as a distinct step, a pattern that scales to future drawdowns too.

03

Phase 2: learning from partner feedback

After the core journey shipped, partner agents reported two points of friction: Open Banking wasn't completing successfully often enough, and agents could pick the wrong payment processor. They also wanted to show merchants an indicative figure before asking them to commit to the full assessment. These weren't isolated interface issues, the assessment page was trying to support different levels of certainty without making the preferred path clear.

Problem Design response Expected benefit
Open Banking wasn't prominent enough Gave it clear primary status and explained why it's preferred. More successful completion, better assessment data.
Agents needed an early indicative figure Created a separate, clearly non-committal estimates calculator. Supports merchant conversations without confusing estimates with assessment.
Processor choice caused errors Dedicated step showing only known, eligible processors. Reduces incorrect selections and decision effort.
Failure could stop progress entirely Offered estimates as a clearly labelled fallback route. Conversation continues without disguising the lower-certainty route.
04

Outcome

What shipped

The core Flex journey shipped and enabled partner agents to [set up Flex accounts and complete first drawdowns within Partner Hub].

What's still pending

The funding assessment improvements were designed in response to partner feedback and documented for implementation. At the time of writing they were not yet live.

RESULT

A shipped 0-to-1 journey used by partner agents in production, plus a validated, ready-to-build redesign of the assessment experience grounded in real usage rather than assumptions made before launch.

05

Reflection

An indicative conversation, an evidence-based assessment and a committed drawdown serve different needs and shouldn't be presented as interchangeable routes. Separating levels of certainty is a design decision, not a detail.

The bigger lesson was about timing: the redesign was stronger because it responded to specific agent behaviour, not assumptions made before launch. With more time, I'd [specific next research or design step], and build a lighter feedback loop into future 0-to-1 launches so this kind of iteration happens sooner.
